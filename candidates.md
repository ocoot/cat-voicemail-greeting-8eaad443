# Candidates — Funniest Cat Voicemail Greeting (max 120 chars)

Judge criteria (in order): (1) clearly a cat's voicemail that lands a laugh; (2) joke specific to cats; (3) clear cat voice/attitude; (4) punch arrives quickly spoken aloud; (5) fresh/surprising, not a tired pun.

## A (106 chars)
Hey, it's Whiskers. I'm busy knocking your glass off the table. Leave a message — I'll ignore it at 4 a.m.
Verdict: Strong cat-specifics (knocking things off, 4 a.m.) and a clear voicemail frame, but "ignore it at 4 a.m." is a slightly muddled punch — the cat ignores the message, or wakes the caller? Loses a beat on clarity.

## B (116 chars)
Hi, you've reached Miso. I'm in the box now. Leave a message after the beep, and yes, I knocked that off on purpose.
Verdict: Perfect voicemail frame, box + knocking-off are both cat-specific, and "on purpose" is a great attitude punch. Slightly long; the punch lands late, after the beep instruction.

## C (125 chars — OVER GATE, rejected on length)
Meow. It's Biscuit. I'm asleep on your keyboard, so leave a message. I'll demand food at 4 a.m. whether you call back or not.
Verdict: Keyboard-sleeping and 4 a.m. food demands are spot-on cat behaviour, but 125 chars fails the 1–120 gate outright, and the second clause drags the punch.

## D (111 chars, trimmed) — WINNER
You've reached Chairman Meow. I'm napping in a tiny box. Leave a message — I'll read it never, then sit on you.
Verdict: Best of the set. "Chairman Meow" gives instant character and attitude; napping in a tiny box is cat-specific and visual; "I'll read it never, then sit on you" is a fast, surprising two-beat punch that lands in the voicemail frame. Gemini mock-judging also ranked this first. Original draft was 122 chars; trimmed "a box smaller than me" → "a tiny box" (111 chars) with no joke lost.

## E (124 chars — OVER GATE, rejected on length)
Hi, it's Pickles. I knocked your vase off, then took a nap in the pieces. Leave a message after the beep. No, I'm not sorry.
Verdict: Fresh image (napping in the shards) and a good unrepentant voice, but 124 chars fails the gate, and "No, I'm not sorry" is a familiar tagline.

## F (129 chars — OVER GATE, rejected on length)
Meow, it's Tuna. I ate your dinner, slept in your clean laundry, and I'm not home. Leave a message. I'll pretend I didn't get it.
Verdict: A list of clichés rather than one sharp joke; the punch is diluted across three clauses, and 129 chars fails the gate.

## Pick
D (111 chars): "You've reached Chairman Meow. I'm napping in a tiny box. Leave a message — I'll read it never, then sit on you."
